Where the money goes, and how you keep it

Not every link is billed the same way. Marina internet is usually a flat monthly charge. Starlink can be unlimited near the coast but charged by the gigabyte further out, or bought in data blocks on business plans. Cellular is charged by the gigabyte. A bill runs away when traffic lands on a metered link and nobody notices: the dish switches to pay-per-GB offshore, the yacht sits in port on 5G, or the crew stream on the SIM. Vistigo shows which link is carrying the traffic and how much data it's using, within minutes, so you can act the same day.

Crew Wi-Fi: one switch, two wins

Each network on board runs on its own VLAN, so Vistigo can split usage and cost between owner, guests and crew. When the crew network is switched off, its bandwidth goes back to the guests and its streaming stops using up metered data. The switch is logged on every graph, so you can see exactly what changed.

  • From the bridge. One button, and the bridge network itself is never switched.
  • On a schedule. Off for guest trips, back on when the yacht is in port.
  • Route-aware cost. Crew traffic is only priced while it's going over cellular.
  • Bandwidth handed back. See the crew's average speed returned to owner and guests.
  • A record of the switch. Every on and off is marked on the timeline.
  • Confirmed by the router. The switch goes through InControl2, so it needs the yacht to be online and takes a few minutes. The dashboard shows when the router has confirmed the change, and alerts the bridge if it hasn't. Usage and cost per network update about once an hour, so the saving shows there within the hour.

Where the numbers come from

Nothing is installed on board. The yacht's Peplink routers report to Peplink's InControl2 cloud service, including what the Starlink dish tells the router, and Vistigo collects from there. The routers need to be registered in InControl2 with an active subscription, and the owner or management company gives Vistigo API access. If the yacht loses internet altogether, readings for that time are missing; an on-board collector that keeps them flowing is an option.
